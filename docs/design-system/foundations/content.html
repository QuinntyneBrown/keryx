Overview

Keryx speaks like a careful colleague: plain, specific and calm. It reports what the collector did in numbers, says exactly what went wrong and what is still fine, and never celebrates or apologises. The reader is staff, not the public.

Voice

  • Specific. “62 of 64 sources fetched · 2 failed”, not “Most sources worked”.
  • Calm. No exclamation marks, no “Oops”, no “Success!”.
  • Plain. “Keryx couldn’t reach its database”, not “A persistence-layer exception occurred”. The technical detail follows in mono for whoever needs it.
  • Accountable. Keryx is the subject of its own failures: “Keryx couldn’t remove the source”, never “You failed to…”.
  • Brief. One idea per sentence. Cut “please”, “simply”, “just”.

Buttons and links

  • Verb first: Run now, Add source, Save changes, Export CSV, Retry failed sources.
  • The confirm button repeats the dialog's verb and object: Remove source, Revoke key.
  • Cancel leaves a dialog; Discard drops edits; Dismiss closes a toast; Close closes a panel.
  • In progress: the same verb with an ellipsis (Adding…, Saving…).
  • Links say where they go: View all 18,432 · All runs · Open original.

ReferenceReference req_8f2c41d0
  • Field errors name the fix and give an example: “Enter a full URL starting with https://, like https://lifepointfit.ca/events.”
  • A screen branches on the error code; the person sees a generic, human message, never a raw server string.
  • Error strings from sources are untrusted and shown as text.

People and sources

  • Keryx aggregates what organisations publish. A person named in a record appears only as the source published them.
  • No private individuals' contact details, anywhere, including logs and error text.
  • Organisation names are written as the organisation writes them.
  • Store facts and a link, not a copy of a source's article: summaries are one or two sentences of Keryx's own.
